# Practice: a reflective map, not a verdict

[Documentation index](../README.md#documentation) · [Deutsche Fassung](practice.de.md)

## 1. Purpose and interpretive boundaries

HDGUI brings planetary positions, zodiac order and Human Design gates into a shared visual workspace. Its practical value is orientation: seeing what a symbol refers to, distinguishing a birth chart from a changing transit, and asking clearer questions. It is not an automated reading or an authority over a person's decisions.

Astrology and Human Design are interpretive traditions, not established scientific accounts of personality, health or life events. Astronomically computed positions do not validate the meanings assigned to them. Personal predictions are not validated by this application. Use the material voluntarily as a reflective vocabulary, not as diagnosis, treatment, a guarantee or a fixed description of someone.

## 2. What the present application offers

The current-transit wheel situates planetary positions in the zodiac and 64-gate sequence; the home BodyGraph shows gates, complete channels and centers derived **only from that transit**. A timestamp and table ground the picture in a particular calculation rather than an undefined “now.” Refreshing the page or enabling autoreload produces new snapshots, not continuous planetary playback.

Saved birth profiles offer a more stable reference. Personality and design are distinct activation sources within the Human Design model, not medically established conscious/unconscious classifications. One selected current profile can highlight gates in the catalog and appear as fixed bands on the sampled timeline. Those bands remain natal references; they do not move with the transit.

The gate/channel/circuit catalog provides a relational vocabulary: a gate connects to harmonic partners through channels, and channels belong to circuits. Short public sample descriptions can be replaced with an authorized local catalog. These texts are interpretive explanations, not measured traits. The gate-walk animation helps learn spatial order and center membership; it follows geometry, not the motion of a planet or the passage of historical time.

## 3. Workflows for study and conversation

**Orient before interpreting.** Open the current-transit view, read its UTC timestamp, choose one body and locate its gate in the catalog. Follow the linked channel and circuit to understand the system's internal relationships. A useful question is “What associations does this vocabulary invite?” rather than “What must happen to me?”

**Compare changing and fixed references.** Create a profile with an accurately known birth time/timezone, select it as current, then enable the natal bands in the gate timeline. Choose a small set of bodies and a manageable interval. Look for overlap or contrast between sampled transit gates and natal references. Channel completion across natal and transit activations can be explored manually as a learning exercise; the application does **not** compute a combined natal–transit chart or automatic synthesis.

**Observe without turning a pattern into a cause.** Keep a journal outside the application, recording the actual observation before consulting symbols. Note context, alternative explanations and examples that do not fit. Return to the timeline later. A meaningful personal association may support reflection, but repetition or visual alignment alone does not establish causation.

**Support an open conversation.** In a voluntary session, let the person choose which themes are useful and reject those that are not. Avoid ranking people by gates, assigning fixed roles or using a chart to decide consent, employment, relationships or healthcare. The current installation supports selecting one profile at a time, not simultaneous multi-person analysis.

## 4. Reading time and uncertainty honestly

The timeline is a sequence of calculated samples; drawn connections are aids to reading, not proof of every intermediate state. Broad windows can overlook brief gate visits, especially with fast-moving bodies. Zooming in or increasing detail helps, but does not remove upstream precision limits.

“Next Gate” and its UTC estimate refer to a computational search for a gate-boundary change, including retrograde motion. They do not forecast a personal event, emotional state or favorable decision window. Short crossings can be missed, searches can fail, and estimates have limited time resolution. Unknown or uncertain birth time also limits confidence in natal references; a visually precise chart should not hide uncertain input.

## 5. Consent, privacy and autonomy

Birth date, time, place and identifying labels deserve care. Obtain informed permission before creating another person's profile, explain what is stored and who can access the host, and agree on deletion and backup retention. Use a minimal label where possible. Local operation reduces transmission to outsiders only when the calculation service is also local; it does not replace access controls or careful handling.

The application has no login or separation between users. Shared settings and the current profile belong to the installation. Do not publish it as a client portal. Deleting a profile does not remove older backups, screenshots or independently shared copies.

Interpretations should remain revisable. Do not present a gate, channel or center as destiny, a deficit, a medical condition or an obligation. Decisions remain with the person, informed by real circumstances and appropriate professional advice where needed.

## 6. Possible future developments — not delivered

A true chronological planetary player could make changing configurations easier to study, with pause, explicit simulated time and selectable speed. This would be different from the existing geometric gate walk. Clear source/sampling labels would be necessary so animation does not imply more precision than the calculations provide.

Optional journaling, annotations and reproducible exports could connect a question, a sampled period and observations without converting associations into evidence. Side-by-side profiles or an explicitly computed natal–transit relationship view could support teaching, provided the source layers, consent and interpretive limitations remain visible. Neither automatic synthesis nor simultaneous multi-profile comparison is currently available.

Other possibilities include accessible non-color distinctions, bilingual interface explanations, transparent alternative authorized catalogs and explicit birth-time uncertainty comparisons. A future interpretation assistant should show the assumptions and source of a suggestion, invite disagreement and avoid deterministic or medical conclusions.

Any shared practice/client workflow would first need authentication, ownership controls, secure access and meaningful retention/export options.
